Website Planning Document

Making biochemistry easier to understand

BioChem Basics will introduce essential biochemical concepts using clear explanations, visual examples, and interactive features designed for students and curious learners.

ATP Energy for life

1. Site Name

BioChem Basics

I chose the name BioChem Basics because it clearly communicates that the website introduces the basic concepts of biochemistry. The name is short, easy to remember, and appropriate for a beginner-friendly educational site.

Optional domain idea: biochembasics.org

2. Site Purpose

The purpose of BioChem Basics is to provide a simple educational introduction to biochemistry. The site will explain how important biological molecules such as proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, enzymes, and ATP work in living organisms. It will also introduce metabolism and show how these concepts are connected to energy and life. The site will use organized information, images, and interactive JavaScript features to make the subject easier for beginners to explore.

3. Scenarios

01

Understanding enzymes

What are enzymes, and how do they help chemical reactions happen in living organisms?

02

Understanding energy

What is ATP, and how is it involved in providing usable energy for cells?

03

Connecting the topics

How do carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, enzymes, and metabolism work together to support life?

5. Typography

Lora

Headings

Lora will be used for major headings and important section titles. Its readable serif style will create a clear visual distinction from body text.

Montserrat

Body and interface text

Montserrat will be used for paragraphs, navigation, buttons, labels, forms, and other interface text because it remains clear at different screen sizes.

7. Planned W06 Site Structure

The final website will contain at least three pages with consistent navigation and visual styling:

  • Home: Introduction to biochemistry and featured topics.
  • Biomolecules: Proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, and enzymes.
  • Energy & Metabolism: ATP, metabolism, and how cells use energy.
  • References: Sources for information and credited images.

The final site will also include an HTML form and JavaScript features such as dynamic topic cards, filtering or selection, conditional messages, arrays and objects, template literals, DOM interaction, events, and localStorage.
