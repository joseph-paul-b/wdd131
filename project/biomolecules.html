The building blocks of life

Understanding Biomolecules

Biomolecules are chemical compounds found in living organisms. They help build cells, store information, provide energy, and support the chemical reactions necessary for life.

1. Proteins

Proteins are large biological molecules made from amino acids. The order of these amino acids helps determine how a protein folds and what it does.

Why are proteins important?

  • Enzymes help speed up chemical reactions.
  • Antibodies help the immune system recognize foreign substances.
  • Collagen supports connective tissues such as skin and tendons.
  • Hemoglobin carries oxygen in red blood cells.

Real-life connection: When you eat eggs, beans, or fish, your digestive system breaks dietary proteins into smaller components that your body can use to build its own proteins.

2. Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates contain carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. They include simple sugars such as glucose and larger molecules such as starch and glycogen.

Why are carbohydrates important?

  • Glucose provides fuel that cells can use to produce ATP.
  • Plants store energy as starch.
  • Animals store glucose in the form of glycogen, especially in the liver and muscles.
  • Some carbohydrates contribute to the structure of cells and tissues.

Real-life connection: When you eat rice, bread, or yam, digestion breaks down many of their carbohydrates into smaller sugars that your body can absorb.

3. Lipids

Lipids are a diverse group of molecules that includes fats, oils, phospholipids, and steroids. Many lipids do not dissolve well in water.

Why are lipids important?

  • Fats store energy for later use.
  • Phospholipids form the main structure of cell membranes.
  • Body fat helps insulate the body and cushion some organs.
  • Cholesterol is used to make certain hormones and other important molecules.

Real-life connection: The membranes surrounding your cells contain lipids that help separate the inside of each cell from its surroundings.

4. Nucleic Acids

Nucleic acids are molecules made from building blocks called nucleotides. The two major examples are DNA and RNA.

Why are nucleic acids important?

  • DNA stores genetic information.
  • RNA helps cells use genetic information to make proteins.
  • Nucleotides also form parts of important molecules, including ATP.

Real-life connection: Your cells use information stored in DNA to help produce the proteins needed for growth, repair, and normal body functions.

Compare the Four Biomolecules

Biomolecule Building Blocks Main Roles
Proteins Amino acids Structure, transport, and enzymes
Carbohydrates Simple sugars Energy and structural support
Lipids Varies by lipid type Energy storage and cell membranes
Nucleic acids Nucleotides Genetic information and its use

How Do Cells Use Energy?

Biomolecules do more than build and maintain living organisms. Cells also transform energy through chemical reactions. Understanding ATP and metabolism helps explain how these reactions support life.
